Moving into a new apartment, the bathroom conditions were top-notch, but there was one disappointing aspect: a bidet wasn't installed. In an era where bidets have become essential home appliances, just having a regular toilet cover sitting there looked very awkward.

I could have called a technician for installation, but to save on call-out fees, I tried **DIY bidet installation**. Instead of a specific brand's product, I used a cost-effective bidet ordered directly from **AliExpress**. Even if it's your first time, there's no need to be intimidated; you can do it very simply.

## 1. Removing the Existing Toilet Cover

The very first thing you need to do is remove the standard toilet cover that is currently installed.
If you feel around the bottom of the toilet (underneath the ceramic bowl), you'll find plastic nuts tightened on both sides.

Even without tools, gripping them tightly by hand and turning them counterclockwise will easily loosen them. After removing the nuts, lift the cover from the top, and it detaches very simply. Wipe the detached area clean with a wet wipe.

## 2. Shutting off the Water Valve and Connecting the T-Valve (Caution Area!)

This is the most important and delicate part of bidet installation.
If you look at the wall next to the toilet, there's an angle valve where water is supplied. **You absolutely must turn this valve all the way to the right to shut off the water.** Otherwise, you might experience the disaster of your bathroom turning into a sea of water.

After shutting off the water, use a wrench to loosen the water supply hose going into the toilet. (It's a good idea to place a towel underneath, as some water trapped in the hose might drip out.)

Connect the **T-valve**, included in the bidet components, to the angle valve side where you disconnected the hose. This part splits the water path into one for the toilet and one for the bidet.

### 💡 The Ups and Downs (Leak Occurrence)
Initially, I roughly tightened the T-valve by hand, gave it a slight turn with the wrench, and opened the valve, but a problem occurred where water dripped out drop by drop from the gaps in the threads. Without panicking, I shut the valve again and removed the T-valve. I meticulously wrapped **Teflon tape (plumber's tape)** three or four times around the thread area and tightened it firmly with the wrench again, which completely fixed the leak. I strongly recommend using Teflon tape when connecting the T-valve!

Having branched the water supply line with a T-valve this time, I think if I need to extend the water supply line when installing a **dishwasher** in the kitchen later, I can just tap into it directly using this same method.

## 3. Installing the Bracket and Mounting the Bidet Main Body

Now that the water work is done, it's 90% complete.
Align the bidet fixing bracket with the holes in the ceramic toilet bowl and secure it tightly using the accessory screws and nuts.

Next, lift the bidet main body and push it in, aligning it with the grooves on the bracket. When you hear a crisp **'Click!'**, it is properly engaged.

## 4. Connecting the Hose and Testing

Finally, connect the remaining hole of the T-valve installed earlier to the bidet main body with the dedicated hose.
Plug the power cord into the outlet and reopen the water valve you had shut.

If the bidet lights up and you hear the sound of water filling up, the installation is a success! Press the wash button and place your hand slightly over it to check if the nozzle operates normally, and all work is finished.

## Conclusion

The difficulty level was such that anyone could do it just by reading the manual carefully. You can create a pleasant bathroom environment with just a little effort, so if you're considering a bidet replacement, it's a great idea to utilize your weekend for a DIY installation.
